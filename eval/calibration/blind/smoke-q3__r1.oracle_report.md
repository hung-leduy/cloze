# Assess the Q3 budget position and the risks to the delivery schedule.

## Executive Summary

Northbridge Analytics' Q3 portfolio position is mixed. Project Zephyr, operating under contract NB-2026-0917 for the Manchester site, remains within its 250,000.00 USD authority, having committed 187,432.55 USD by 20 August 2026, leaving 62,567.45 USD held against remaining milestones. This project is nonetheless the portfolio's principal concern: revenue fell twelve percent against plan following cancellation of the Leeds pilot and a slipped Bristol fulfilment migration, prompting the board to defer launch to 2027 pending a revised plan due 30 September 2026. Project Aurora, under contract NB-2026-1044, sits comfortably within its 120,000.00 USD authority (98,210.00 USD spent) but faces a supplier-driven schedule risk, with Calder Logistics falling 340 units short of its July commitment. Pilot Kestrel presents the clearest budget concern, having overspent its 45,000.00 USD authority by 6,237.75 USD, a high-severity issue requiring immediate remediation. Overall, schedule slippage and vendor underperformance pose greater near-term risk than raw spend levels.

## Budget Position by Project

Across the three active initiatives, spend against authority shows a mixed picture heading into the 30 September 2026 review.

Project Zephyr, operating under contract NB-2026-0917 for the Manchester site, holds an authorised budget of 250,000.00 USD, against which 187,432.55 USD had been committed by 20 August 2026. This leaves an unspent balance of 62,567.45 USD, which is explicitly held against remaining milestones and is not scheduled for release before the September review. Zephyr therefore remains within authority, with headroom, but that headroom is earmarked rather than discretionary.

Project Aurora, under contract NB-2026-1044, shows an authorised budget of 120,000.00 USD with 98,210.00 USD spent, leaving 21,790.00 USD unspent. Aurora's spend position is sound; its exposure lies instead in delivery performance rather than fiscal standing, as discussed further below.

Pilot Kestrel presents the one clear budget breach: against an authorised amount of 45,000.00 USD, spend has reached 51,237.75 USD, an overrun of 6,237.75 USD. This is the only project currently operating outside its authorised envelope, and it has been flagged as a high-severity risk by its owner, Priya Ramanathan.

In aggregate, of the three projects, two remain within authorised limits with meaningful unspent balances, while one has already exceeded its ceiling. The combined authorised budget across the portfolio is 415,000.00 USD, against combined spend of 336,880.30 USD, but this blended view masks the fact that Kestrel's overrun requires a distinct remediation response rather than absorption within portfolio-level headroom.

## Contractual and Spend Authority Context

Each project's spending flexibility is defined by its contract instrument and the residual balance held against outstanding milestones. Project Zephyr operates under contract NB-2026-0917, covering the Manchester site, with an authorised ceiling of 250,000.00 USD. As of 20 August 2026, 187,432.55 USD had been committed, leaving an unspent balance of 62,567.45 USD. This balance is explicitly held against remaining milestones and is not released before the 30 September 2026 review, meaning no incremental spending authority is available for Zephyr in the interim regardless of the Leeds cancellation.

Project Aurora is governed by a separate instrument, contract NB-2026-1044, which the board confirmed is unaffected by the Leeds decision. With 98,210.00 USD spent against a 120,000.00 USD authority, Aurora retains 21,790.00 USD of headroom, which the board intends to absorb the Calder Logistics shortfall of 340 units without requiring a contract amendment.

Pilot Kestrel presents a different constraint: spend of 51,237.75 USD already exceeds its 45,000.00 USD authority under contract NB-2026-1187, an overrun of 6,237.75 USD. This breach means Kestrel has no remaining authorised capacity and requires either a formal budget amendment or contract revision before further expenditure can proceed, distinguishing it materially from Zephyr and Aurora's constrained-but-intact positions.

## Delivery Schedule Risks

The delivery schedule for Q3 faces three distinct operational threats, each recorded independently across the quarterly review, steering memo, and board minutes.

First, the cancellation of the Leeds pilot in August 2026 has directly undermined Project Zephyr, with quarterly revenue falling twelve percent against plan. The steering committee attributed this shortfall not merely to the cancellation itself but to the delayed migration of the Bristol fulfilment centre, which had been scheduled for completion before the quarter closed but slipped past quarter end. This dual failure—a cancelled pilot compounded by a missed migration milestone—has materially weakened the delivery position for Zephyr's remaining milestones, which the unspent contract balance is being held against pending the 30 September review.

Second, vendor performance has introduced a discrete risk to Project Aurora. Calder Logistics missed the July commitment by 340 units, a shortfall Daniel Okoye formally logged against Aurora rather than treating as a knock-on effect of the Leeds decision. The board was assured that Aurora's contract (NB-2026-1044) would absorb this delay without requiring contractual amendment, suggesting the shortfall, while flagged as medium severity, is not currently viewed as schedule-critical.

Third, Pilot Kestrel presents a compounding risk where financial overrun signals possible operational strain: spend has exceeded authorised budget by 6,237.75 USD, prompting Priya Ramanathan to request a revised plan. While not explicitly tied to a schedule slippage, an overrun of this scale on a small-authority pilot raises questions about scope control that could affect delivery timing if unaddressed before the September review.

## Governance Decisions and Escalations

The board's response to the Q3 pressures was formalised at its meeting of 28 August 2026, chaired by Sarah Whitfield with Daniel Okoye and Priya Ramanathan in attendance. Following review of Project Zephyr in light of the cancelled Leeds pilot, the board resolved unanimously, on a motion from Priya Ramanathan, to defer the launch to 2027 and to table a revised plan at the 30 September 2026 meeting. This decision formalises the recommendation first raised by Sarah Whitfield in the June review and reiterated in the steering memo of 22 August 2026, giving leadership a further reporting cycle to reassess milestones before committing the remaining balance under contract NB-2026-0917.

On Project Aurora, Daniel Okoye escalated the Calder Logistics shortfall of 340 units against the July commitment but confirmed it would be absorbed within existing delivery plans, with no change to contract NB-2026-1044 and no read-across from the Leeds cancellation.

Pilot Kestrel's overrun of 6,237.75 USD prompted Priya Ramanathan to request a revised plan, though the board did not authorise additional spend at this meeting, leaving the project's budget status unresolved pending further review. Collectively, these decisions indicate a governance posture of deferral and containment rather than immediate remediation, with substantive resolution deferred to the 30 September meeting.

## Outlook and Recommendations

Ahead of the 30 September 2026 review, three actions warrant priority. First, Project Zephyr's unspent balance of 62,567.45 USD against contract NB-2026-0917 should remain ring-fenced against the remaining milestones rather than reallocated, given the board's confirmation that the launch is deferred to 2027 and a revised plan is pending; any drawdown ahead of the review would pre-empt that decision. Second, Pilot Kestrel's overrun of 6,237.75 USD against its 45,000 USD authority requires an immediate contract amendment or supplementary authorisation under NB-2026-1187; continuing to spend without a corrected ceiling exposes the board to further unauthorised commitments and should be resolved before, not at, the September meeting. Third, Project Aurora's absorption of the 340-unit Calder Logistics shortfall without a change to NB-2026-1044 should be monitored closely over the next quarter, as its 98,210 USD spend against a 120,000 USD authority leaves limited headroom if the vendor shortfall recurs or compounds delays already affecting the Bristol migration.

More broadly, the twelve percent revenue shortfall tied to the cancelled Leeds pilot and delayed Bristol migration underscores the need for the revised Zephyr plan to include realistic milestone dates and contingency for vendor performance. The board should require written confirmation from Calder Logistics on remediation before the next review and treat Pilot Kestrel's overspend as a template for tighter authority controls across active contracts.
